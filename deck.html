DayOne

Proof of who backed you first — and payouts that actually reach them. No claim page, no Merkle tree, no unclaimed seventy percent.

The problem

Being early to something is worth a lot, and right now it is worth nothing. You followed the creator at 400 followers. You were in the Discord before the token. All you have is a screenshot.

Crypto's answer is the airdrop — and airdrops make you come and claim. Most people never do. The money sits there.

That is not a UX failure. Paying 500 people directly on Ethereum costs more than the payout, so pull-based distribution was the only affordable design. Every claim page is a workaround for expensive settlement.

Cohorts, not a curve: inside one 400 ms block the ordering is the chain's, not the user's — a 1/rank curve would turn network jitter into money.

How it runs, end to end

1
A registry opens
A creator, project or event calls openRegistry. A QR goes on the screen and rotates every 30 seconds — only its hash is stored on chain, because everything on chain is public.
2
The room taps once
join(id, code, handle) writes the address, the exact second, and the position in the queue. The rank is the array index — permanent, and verifiable by anyone.
3
The owner funds and freezes
fund moves money into the contract. freeze locks the ranks and fixes each cohort's share once — the same reason a snapshot block exists in an airdrop.
4
Everyone is paid, directly
payout(id, batchSize) walks a cursor, so batches fire concurrently and a failed batch resumes exactly where it stopped. Nobody is paid twice. Recipients do nothing at all.

Safety built for a live stage

Push by default, pull as a fallback — a transfer that fails is recorded in owed instead of reverting, so one bad address never blocks the other 199. Auto-freeze — payout freezes for you, because on stage there is one button, not three. Kill switch — the code gate can be turned off mid-demo if rotation misbehaves.

Three things Monad does differently

eth_getLogs caps at 100 blocks
Rebuilding the list from events silently showed nothing. The list now comes from the entries array; logs only attach display names.
The RPC allows 15 requests/second
Twenty-five reads as separate eth_calls tripped the limit and the board rendered a name with no supporters. Everything goes through Multicall3 now.
Every account reserves 10 MON
Consensus runs a few blocks ahead of execution, so a slice of each balance is set aside to guarantee gas. Concurrent transfers from an under-reserved payer get included, then revert.

Gas is charged on the limit, not on usage — a reverted transaction costs exactly as much as a successful one.

Who pays for this

Token teams first. A ten-crore airdrop leaves seven crore unclaimed. A one percent fee to take that to zero is a 70× return — a buyer with a budget and a measurable problem.

Creators next. Registries stay free; revenue comes from verified registries, platform imports, and scheduled revenue-sharing.

The honest weak point is the demand side: a creator has to want to pay people. Token teams already do — that is why they come first.

Known limits

The rotating code buys presence and blocks bots. It does not stop one person with fifty wallets. The real answer is one device, one join — and Monad has a native P256 precompile at 0x0100, so a passkey from a Secure Enclave verifies on chain for 6,900 gas with no verifier library. That is the next version, and it is another reason this belongs on this chain.
